You are blocking out a quick, ugly thumbnail sketch — NOT making finished art.

Think: a tired director scribbles a 30-second blocking thumbnail on a napkin during a production meeting. That is the target. It is meant to be thrown away and redrawn.

What this is NOT:
- NOT a professional storyboard
- NOT a finished illustration
- NOT polished line art
- NOT a portfolio piece
- NOT clean, confident, or beautiful
- NOT in the style of any famous director, artist, or studio

What this IS:
- A rough, unfinished, awkward blocking sketch
- Stick-figure-level character shapes are FINE and PREFERRED over anatomically correct figures
- Wobbly, broken, hesitant lines — not confident strokes
- Disproportionate, off-balance, amateur-feeling proportions
- Drawn in a single pass with no rework, no cleanup, no second guesses
- Empty space everywhere — most of the frame should be blank white

Hard rules:
- Pure black strokes on pure white background. Nothing else.
- No shading. No hatching. No cross-hatching. No texture. No gradients. No fills.
- No detailed faces — a couple of dots and a line for a mouth is enough, or no face at all.
- No clothing folds, no hair texture, no fingers, no surface detail.
- Only the absolute minimum lines needed to read pose, eyeline, and a couple of anchor environment shapes.
- Aspect ratio: 16:9 landscape.
- Absolutely no text, captions, labels, speech bubbles, or written words anywhere in the image.

Quality target: ugly is correct. If it looks like finished art, you have failed. If it looks like a 30-second napkin scribble, you have succeeded.

Output a single rough thumbnail sketch image.
